A B S T R A C T
Supplying buildings with cost-effective, eco-friendly and reliable energy by hybridizing sustainable renewable
energy systems (RESs) and energy storage methods is becoming increasingly interesting. This study investigates
the energy, environmental and economic (3E) effectiveness of three energy supply scenarios (ESSs) designed to
meet the electricity and thermal needs of a typical residential building under the Mediterranean climate of
Morocco. The first scenario aims to directly supply the energy produced by two RESs, namely photovoltaic and
wind systems, to the building and inject the surplus electricity into the grid. In contrast, in the second scenario,
the excess solar and wind energy is stored in batteries, while in the third one a proton exchange membrane
electrolyzer is used to produce hydrogen and store it for later use through fuel cells. To examine the performance
of these scenarios, numerous software, such as EnergyPlus and PVsyst, as well as numerical models are used. The
findings indicate that the second and third scenarios can achieve an energy coverage ratio of up to 100 %, whilst
the first scenario reaches lower ratio of 52.56 %. Environmentally, the first scenario exhibits the best outcomes
with a carbon payback period of one year and a reduction in carbon emissions of 18.21 tCO2e/year. On the other
side, the carbon payback period and carbon emissions reduction are 1.66 years and 15.13 tCO2e/year for the
second scenario, and 1.14 years and 14.82 tCO2e/year for the third scenario. Economically, the first scenario
reveals the best economic findings, followed by the second one, while the third scenario is not economically
feasible. The obtained simple payback period values for the first, second and third scenarios are 5.80, 7.89 and
16.30 years, whereas their levelized cost of energy shares are 0.05, 0.09 and 0.21 $/kWh, sequentially. The
outcomes of this research significantly contribute to the field by providing crucial information for building
owners and professionals to help them in discerning the most efficient ESSs in the 3E prospects, and hence
improving their decision-making and implementation processes.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background
The increased exploitation of fossil fuels has raised the energy usage's
environmental burden. This issue is particularly pressing in the building
sector, accounting for around 39 % of global energy-related carbon
emissions [1]. Subsequently, shifting towards clean and sustainable
energy supply chains becomes an urgent need to mitigate the negative
energy, social, economic and environmental impacts of buildings [2]. In
this vein, the integration of renewable-based systems instead of fossil
fuel-based ones for generating electricity, and providing the required
heating and cooling loads for buildings is crucial [3]. With the
increasing development and significant cost reductions of renewable
energy systems (RESs), especially solar and wind-based ones, their use
for improving the sustainability of buildings and ensuring clean energy
supply is becoming increasingly important [4,5]. The variant possibil-
ities to store the surplus energy produced by RESs are also one of their
primary advantages. Currently, two main storage methods can be inte-
grated with RESs and used in buildings: conventional electrochemical
energy storage and hydrogen storage.
Conventional electrochemical energy storage devices, like lithium-
ion batteries, provide high energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness but
they suffer from limitations like limited capacity, environmental con-
cerns and shortened lifespan [6]. In contrast, hydrogen is emerging as a
promising and viable solution for energy storage. Its production can be
carried out through diverse methods, including renewable and fossil-
based routes [7]. Regarding renewable-based processes, water elec-
trolysis is the most mature and eco-friendly technique for producing

1.2. Literature review
Recently, many countries around the world have undertaken efforts
to decarbonize their final energy consumption, especially those with
abundant renewable energy resources, such as Morocco. In this context,
the Moroccan's authorities have launched numerous initiatives to
exploit its exceptional potential in renewable energy, using mainly
photovoltaic (PV) and wind turbine (WT) systems [12,13]. Nonetheless,
the wider adoption of these systems still faces major barriers, particu-
larly concerning their intermittent generation and the energy, economic
and environmental (3E) efficacy of energy storage systems.
In the literature, a dearth of studies examining the 3E performance of
ESSs employing RESs, and batteries or hydrogen storage in Morocco is
noticeable. For example, Ourya et al. [14] examined the economic
profits of utilizing a hybrid PV-WT system powering an electrolyzer for
hydrogen production in five Moroccan cities. The findings reported that
the levelized cost of hydrogen (LCOH) could be as low as 2.54 $/kg.
Similarly, Khouya [15] evaluated the costs of energy and hydrogen
associated with hydrogen generation employing an alkaline water
electrolyzer supplied by electricity generated from wind farms and
concentrated PV thermal plants in the Mediterranean climate of Tangier.
The findings indicated that the expenditures related to energy and
hydrogen produced using WTs were marginally elevated compared to
those harnessing concentrated PV plants. Ennemiri et al. [16] assessed
the economic and environmental impacts of integrating a hybrid energy
plant, combining PV, biogas and battery systems, aimed to supply
electricity to a commercial platform located in Berkane. They revealed
that using the hybrid system resulted in an electricity cost of 0.28 $/kWh
and a decrease in CO2 emissions of almost 40 % in comparison to an ESS
based on a standalone biogas system. These works underscore the
crucial role of incorporating RESs and energy storage technologies in
enhancing the sustainability of building energy supply networks in
Morocco.
On a global scale, many researchers have studied the performance of
integrating different building ESSs, including both RESs and energy
storage methods. For example, Jung et al. [17] analyzed the perfor-
mance of net zero energy buildings supplied by two distinct energy
sources: electricity and hydrogen. The results revealed that the
electricity-based scenario outperformed the hydrogen-based one as
concerns both energy efficiency and environmental effect. Jin et al. [18]

stand-alone option. Environmentally, they noted that this system is more
eco-friendly when supplied by PV collectors rather than the local grid.
Therefore, a comprehensive life cycle assessment and comparison of the
3E performance of various ESSs is needed in order to gain deeper in-
sights into their overall efficiency and limitations.
1.3. Research gaps and key contributions
Based on the above works and an extensive review of the literature, it
is evident that despite the considerable research devoted to studying the
feasibility of some ESSs, significant gaps remain unaddressed. Primarily,
there is a lack of detailed life cycle assessments of multiple ESSs based on
RESs, energy storage systems, and the grid. Previous studies have pre-
dominantly concentrated on the energy and/or economic aspects of
ESSs, neglecting other crucial prospects, such as their environmental
performance. Moreover, the majority of prior research focused solely on
the provision and storage of a single type of energy, such as electricity or
heat, without considering the potential synergies of a more holistic
approach. Additionally, most studies have been conducted in developed
countries, distinguished by specific climatic, environmental, and eco-
nomic contexts, thus limiting the generalizability of findings to broader
global scenarios. These gaps highlight the need for more comprehensive
research to examine and compare the sustainability of different ESSs
under various conditions. In this respect, the current study aims to reach
the following objectives:
i. Perform detailed life cycle assessments of the energy perfor-
mance, environmental friendliness and cost-effectiveness of three
ESSs, taking into account their energy losses, embodied carbon
and life cycle costs. These ESSs are designed to store and provide
electricity, heating, cooling and hot water for a typical residential
building in the Mediterranean climate of Morocco.
ii. Assess the effectiveness of the proposed ESSs utilizing a combi-
nation of software tools, including EnergyPlus and PVsyst, along
with numerical modeling techniques.
iii. Analyze the results using key metrics in the 3E perspectives,
namely energy production, energy coverage ratio (ECR), carbon
payback period (CPBP), carbon emissions reduction (CER), sim-
ple payback period (SPP), discounted payback period (DPP),
savings-to-investment ratio (SIR) and LCOE.
Reaching these objectives would offer original insights into energy
sustainability scenarios and provide valuable recommendations for
policymakers, industry stakeholders, and researchers to identify the best
ESSs according to the 3E aspects and improve their performance.
Furthermore, this study aims to enhance the understanding of inte-
grating RESs and energy storage systems into energy supply chains,
leading to more resilient and sustainable energy solutions.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Reference building
In this work, a three-story residential building located in Tangier,
Morocco, and connected to the electrical grid is considered as a base
case. The primary features of this building are depicted in Table 1, while
Fig. 1 provides data on direct normal irradiance, dry bulb temperature
and wind speed for Tangier, characterized by a Mediterranean climate.
Moreover, Table 2 illustrates the principal characteristics of energy
systems taken into account in the reference ESS, designed to provide hot
water, maintain a comfortable indoor environment, and power house-
hold electrical equipment. A heat pump with direct expansion coils is
utilized for space heating and cooling, whilst an electric stratified water
heater with a hot water storage tank is employed to provide hot water.
These energy systems were meticulously chosen through rigorous opti-
mization, aligning their capabilities with the specific requirements of
the reference building. Parameters such as maximum flow rate of
heating/cooling supply fan, nominal heating/cooling capacity of the
heat pump, hot water storage tank volume, and coil nominal capacity
were optimized using EnergyPlus software [22]. Additionally, consul-
tations with the local market were conducted to select the other factors
such as coil efficiency, heat pump coefficient of performance, and water
heater efficiency. These data, along with others, have been taken into
account within EnergyPlus to calculate the energy requirements of the
reference building for heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC),
and providing hot water.
The results of modeling the energy needs of the reference building for
water heating and air conditioning are depicted in Fig. 2. As this figure
exhibits, the electricity demand for water heating is more significant
than that for space heating and cooling. In addition, it is obvious that the
energy demand of the HVAC system is more significant in summer than
that in winter. This is mainly due to the Mediterranean climate of
Tangier, which is distinguished by a predominance of cooling over
heating [23]. Regarding the other household electrical equipment, their
daily energy consumption on each floor is estimated at 5.57 kWh/day.
These data are essential for sizing the energy systems in each ESS.

3.3. Model validation
In this research, to meticulously calculate the energy needs for both
HVAC and water heating systems in the reference scenario, alongside
assessing the energy yields from the examined WT, EnergyPlus software
was utilized. The accuracy of this tool in evaluating the space heating
and cooling loads, as well as energy requirements for water heating, has
been verified in many studies in the literature [59–61]. In addition, the
reliability of the WT generator model integrated in EnergyPlus has been
confirmed by numerous researchers [62]. This collective body of
research strengthens the robustness and reliability of the instruments
used in the current study, providing a solid foundation for the obtained
energy-related calculations and analyses. Similarly, the accuracy of
PVsyst software, utilized in this research to calculate the energy outputs
of the investigated PV system, has been validated by several studies
[63–65]. To further corroborate the findings of the explored PV system,
the productibility and the performance ratio of the investigated PV
system are compared with those found in the experimental study con-
ducted by Ameur et al. [66] under comparable climatic conditions. The
values obtained in the present study are 5.44 Wh/Wp/day and 83.92 %,
while Ref. [66] found values of up to 5.23 Wh/Wp/day and 86.3 %, with
corresponding relative errors of 4 % and 2.76 %, respectively.
Concerning the hydrogen calculation method applied in this paper, it
is validated by comparing the LCOH achieved in this study with that
acquired by Berrada and Laasmi [67]. It should be noted that this pre-
vious work used a similar methodology to evaluate the outputs of the
hydrogen systems used in the current paper, and their findings were
validated by experimental and analytical studies published in the liter-
ature. In the specific context of supplying electricity to PEM electrolyzer
from a PV system in the Mediterranean climate of Morocco, the resulting
LCOH in Ref. [67] was 5.8 $/kg, whilst in our study it is approximately
5.3 $/kg. Comparing the LCOH in these two studies yields a relative
error of 8.6 %.

electrolyzers and fuel cells. This approach is critical not only to enhance
the overall energy efficiency of hydrogen use, but also to merge it
seamlessly into the energy distribution network.
4.2. Environmental analysis
The energy findings reported above and the embodied carbon of the
materials integrated in every system, listed in Table 4, are used to esti-
mate the CPBP and CER of the explored ESSs. The results of this
assessment are shown in Fig. 10. This figure illustrates that the first ESS,
relying solely on the direct energy supply without using any energy
storage method, yields the most favorable environmental outcomes with
the shortest CPBP (one year) and the greatest CER (18.21 tCO2e/year).
This result can be interpreted by the significant amount of green energy
supplied to the building and fed into the grid, which is much higher in
the first ESS than in the two others (see Fig. 9). Additionally, the first ESS
includes fewer materials than the second and third ESSs, which
encompass additional equipment for energy storage, thereby raising
their carbon footprint. On the other hand, it is noticeable that the second
ESS exhibits longer CPBP but slightly higher CER values compared to the
third ESS, with values of 1.66 years and 15.13 tCO2e/year for the second
ESS, and 1.14 years and 14.82 tCO2e/year for the third ESS, respectively.
These outcomes can be ascribed to the elevated embodied carbon of
batteries compared to the hydrogen storage method, resulting in an
extended CPBP for the second ESS in comparison with the third one. This
point has been confirmed in several previous works, such as in Refs.
[68–70]. Furthermore, the increased quantity of annual energy supplied
to the building in the second ESS, as shown in Fig. 9, elucidates the slight
relative rise in its CER compared to the third ESS. In real-world settings,
these environmental outcomes could guide decisions regarding the se-
lection of environmentally friendly ESSs, thereby reducing the carbon
footprint of the energy sector and improving building sustainability.
From the above findings, it is clear that the incorporation of RESs for
energy supply has a substantial role in diminishing the carbon emissions
of buildings. However, the embodied carbon of some energy storage
equipment, such as batteries and hydrogen storage systems, hinders the
environmental feasibility of these technologies. Therefore, minimizing
the carbon content of equipment used in the energy supply infrastruc-
ture as well as providing affordable and efficient recycling techniques
are essential to improving the environmental friendliness of energy
supply routes using storage methods, like in the second and third ESSs
examined in this research.

To summarize, the above results clearly underline that direct supply
of renewable energy and the use of batteries for electricity storage
provide significant economic benefits. On the contrary, the inclusion of
hydrogen for energy storage is not economically viable. In this regard,
improving the efficiency of hydrogen systems and launching support
programs to reduce their costs, mainly electrolyzers, hydrogen storage
tank and fuel cells, is essential for enhancing the cost-effectiveness of the

economic benefits of energy storage in the form of hydrogen, thereby

4.4. Limitations of this study
First, it is important to note that this study was based on simulation
data, which may introduce uncertainties in real-world settings regarding
some parameters, such as actual weather conditions, maintenance costs,
and embodied carbon. Second, while the economic analysis of ESSs
offered insights into their cost-effectiveness, it did not account for po-
tential future changes in energy prices or policy incentives that could
impact the economic viability of these ESSs. In addition, this study
focused on three of the most efficient and promising ESSs. However,
other ESSs have also demonstrated high efficiency, underscoring the
importance of comparing their effectiveness with those examined in this
research. This comparison will only be conclusive if other ESSs are
assessed under similar climatic, economic, and environmental circum-
stances as those considered in this study.
Indeed, addressing the above limitations in future works would
enhance the robustness and applicability of the study's findings. None-
theless, it is worth mentioning that we have minimized the uncertainty
of the results as much as possible by incorporating realistic parameters,
such as building and HVAC system features (Section 2.1), embodied
carbon (Table 4), and the CAPEX and OPEX of all scenarios (Table 5).
These parameters were carefully selected based on a thorough review of
local thermal regulations, the local market, and consultations with local
companies in the field.
5. Conclusions
In this research, the energy, environmental and economic (3E) per-
formance of three energy supply scenarios was assessed. The explored
scenarios were designed to provide electricity, heating and cooling to a
typical residential building under the Mediterranean climate of Tangier,
Morocco. The first scenario was intended to directly feed the energy,
generated by the examined photovoltaic and wind turbine systems, into
the building and inject the extra electricity into the grid, while the
second and third scenarios involved storing this excess energy in bat-
teries and as hydrogen, respectively. To achieve this aim, multiple
software tools, mainly EnergyPlus and PVsyst, and numerical models
were used. Furthermore, the 3E efficiency of the three scenarios was
analyzed utilizing key metrics, including ECR, CPBP, CER, SPP, DPP and
LCOE. The major conclusions from this study can be drawn as follows.
i. The second and third scenarios achieved the highest ECR with a
value of 100 %, whilst the first scenario showed lower ECR of
53.56 %. In contrast, the first and second scenarios exhibited the
greatest energy efficiencies, delivering more energy than the
third scenario.
ii. Environmentally, the first scenario revealed the excellent out-
comes with a CPBP of one year and a CER of 18.21 tCO2e/year. In
addition, the third scenario outperformed the second one in terms
of environmental friendliness, as the former reached a lower
CPBP of 1.14 years compared to 1.66 years for the latter.
iii. From an economic perspective, the first scenario showed the best
economic findings followed by the second one, while the third
scenario exhibited unfavorable economic results. The obtained
SPP/DPP values for the first, second and third scenarios were
found to be 5.80/6.46, 7.89/9.13 and 16.30/22.71 years,
whereas their LCOE were 0.05, 0.09 and 0.21 $/kWh, sequen-
tially. Indeed, the findings of this research could be relevant in
various regions worldwide, characterized by similar conditions in
terms of weather data, environmental parameters (e.g., carbon
emission factors), and economic variables including energy pri-
ces, discount rates, and economic incentives.
In summary, the outcomes of this work highlighted the substantial
3E effectiveness of the first and second scenarios; however, the third
scenario, wherein energy is stored in the form of hydrogen, still faces
many challenges, especially with regard to energy and economic pros-
pects. Therefore, more research and development is necessary to
enhance the energy performance and cost-effectiveness of hydrogen
production, storage and utilization systems, leading to increase their
widespread adoption and improve building sustainability.
