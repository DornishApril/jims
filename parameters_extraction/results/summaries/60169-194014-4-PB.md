## SECTION 4: POWER GENERATION ALGORITHM -- EXACT EXTRACTION

### 4.1 Dispatch Strategy

**Type of dispatch:** Priority-based multi-tier dispatch (novel MOPSO-based control)

**Priority order:**
1. PV + Wind (renewable sources) -- first to meet load directly
2. Battery -- discharged when RES insufficient
3. Fuel Cell -- uses stored hydrogen to fill remaining deficit
4. Diesel Generator -- absolute last resort (missing load after all above)

**Decision logic (5 cases):**
- Case 1: P_PV + P_WT > P_load --> excess goes to battery first, then to electrolyzer for H2 production; FC off
- Case 2: P_PV + P_WT > P_load + battery full + H2 tank full --> surplus is dumped
- Case 3: P_load > P_PV + P_WT --> battery discharges to fill deficit
- 4: P_load > P_PV + P_WT + P_BES --> FC turns on: P_FC = P_load - P_PV - P_WT - P_BES
- Case 5: P_load > P_PV + P_WT + P_FC + P_BES --> DG covers missing load

**Constraints on component operation:**
- Battery SOC: 20% <= SOC <= 100%
- H2 tank SOC: 5% <= SOC <= 100%

### 4.2 Power Flow Logic

**Excess renewable energy handling:**
- Step 1: Charge battery (until SOC_Bmax / fully charged)
- Step 2: If battery full, send to electrolyzer to produce hydrogen --> stored in tank
- Step 3: If both battery and H2 tank are full, surplus is recorded as excess energy (available for HDI calculation)

**Deficit handling:**
- Step 1: Battery discharges (to minimum SOC limit of 20%)
- Step 2: FC activates using stored hydrogen (to minimum 5% SOC limit)
- Step 3: DG as absolute last resort (in this study: never activated)

**Battery charging/discharging logic:**
- SOC_B(t) = SOC_B(t-1) * (1-sigma) + (P_T(t) - P_inv(t)) * eta_BC [charging]
- SOC_B(t) = SOC_B(t-1) * (1-sigma) - (P_inv(t) - P_T(t)) * eta_BC [discharging]
- SOCmin = DOD * SOC_nominal; SOCmax = SOC_nominal

**Hydrogen production logic:**
- Electrolyzer runs when excess exists AND battery is at capacity
- m_dot_H2 = eta_EZ * P_EZ / HHV_H2
- Tank capacity: C_HT(t) = C_HT(t-1) + m_dot_H2(t) - L_dot_H2(t)

**Diesel generator start/stop:**
- Starts: P_PV + P_PT + P_FC + P_BES < P_load
- In this study: never activated

**Grid interaction:** None (fully off-grid system)

### 4.4 Algorithm Flow

The energy management system runs an hourly loop (8,760 hours/year). At each time step t:

1. Read input data (load demand, solar irradiance, wind speed, component specs)
2. Calculate P_PV(t) and P_WT(t) from irradiance/wind using温度和功率曲线模型
3. If P_PV + P_WT >= P_load:
   - Supply load from renewables
   - Compute P_ch(t) for battery charging
   - If Ech(t) <= (Ebmax - Eb(t-1)): charge battery; set Eb(t) = Eb(t-1) + Ech(t)
   - Else: battery is full, define surplus for electrolyzer
   - Loop 8,760 times
4. If P_PV + P_WT < P_load:
   - Compute P_distch(t) = (P_load / eta_inv) - P_PV(t) - P_WT(t)
   - If Eb(t-1) - Ebmin >= Edistch(t): discharge battery; Eb(t) = Eb(t-1) - Edisch(t)
   - Else: need additional source; run FC to meet deficient load (P_FC = P_load - P_PV - P_WT - P_FC)
   - If P_PV + P_WT + P_FC + P_BES < P_load: run DG
5. Record all component outputs, SOC states, energy flows, excess

## SECTION 5: ANALYTICAL INSIGHT AND SYSTEM EXPLANATION (YOUR ANALYSIS)

### 5.1 Power Generation Walkthrough

**The Koundoul system is designed around a 542,390 kWh/year annual load (average ~1,486 kWh/day, peak 144 kW in the evening).**

**Morning (6 AM - 12 PM):**
As solar irradiance rises from zero (sunrise ~6 AM), PV output ramps rapidly. In peak conditions, PV reaches 323 kW output (vs rated 493 kW due to temperature/wind conditions -- approximately 65.5% capacity factor at midday). Wind during morning hours produces modest output around 32-104 kW depending on the day. Combined PV+W frequently exceeds the daytime load (average ~91 kW), generating substantial surplus. Every midday, the surplus of approximately (323+104-91) = ~336 kW during peak hours is first directed to charging the battery, then to the electrolyzer (202 kW rated) for hydrogen production.

**Midday (12 PM - 2 PM):**
Peak solar irradiance reaches 1,017 W/m2, driving PV to its maximum 323 kW. Wind peaks at 104 kW around 10 AM but may moderate by midday. Total RES output (~350-427 kW) against a load of ~91 kW produces ~260-336 kW of excess power. The battery absorbs as much as its charging rate allows; remaining excess drives the electrolyzer at up to 202 kW input, producing green hydrogen at approximately eta_EZ * P_EZ / HHV_H2 = 0.74 * 202 / 33.33 = ~4.48 kg/hour of H2 under full electrolyzer load. Given that the annual H2 energy stored is 83,874 kWh (~2,516 kg), this production occurs primarily during high-solar months (November-May when battery also has spare charging capacity).

**Evening (6 PM - 10 PM):**
This is the critical period. Solar drops to zero after 6 PM. Wind provides partial output (typically 32-104 kW). Meanwhile, load peaks at **144 kW** between 6-10 PM -- creating a deficit of potentially 112-144 kW that must be entirely met from storage. The battery is dispatched first per the control strategy (Case 3): it discharges up to its available energy (SOC_min=20% constraint). Annual battery discharge of 221,300 kWh translates to ~606 kWh/day on average. If battery is depleted (SOC hits 20%), the fuel cell activates (Case 4): rated 144 kW, it converts stored hydrogen back to electricity at 50% efficiency. The fuel cell contributes 41,937 kWh/year -- about 115 kWh/day average -- primarily during these extended deficit periods.

**Night (10 PM - 1 AM):**
Load diminishes. Wind may still be available. Battery continues discharging if needed. The system enters a lower-power state.

**Overnight (1 AM - 7:30 AM):**
Load is zero (paper explicitly states no consumption 1:00 AM to 7:30 AM). All wind and any little solar (none) is directed to battery charging and hydrogen production. This "free charging window" of 7.5 hours/day allows battery recovery.

**Seasonal variations:**
- Solar is dominant throughout the year (83% of RES generation) due to high irradiance at this Sahelian location
- Wind production drops July-November (rainy season); during these months hydrogen storage becomes more critical
- Hydrogen contribution appears mainly May-November: this is when solar surpluses after battery charging are largest, enabling electrolyzer operation
- Battery contributes year-round (~229,180 kWh charged annually = ~628 kWh/day average)

### 5.2 System Behavior Analysis

**Why this configuration is optimal:**
The Pareto front reveals a fundamental trade-off: higher SSSI (more self-sufficiency) requires larger component sizes (higher cost). The MOPSO algorithm identified 493 kW PV + 166 kW wind + 229,180 kWh battery + 202 kW electrolyzer + 997 H2 tanks + 144 kW FC as the balanced optimal point (SSSI 51.14%, ASC $156,033). Notably, the DG is zero -- the system achieves 100% renewable fraction at lower cost than Chad's national utility production ($0.2982 vs $0.61/kWh).

**Why PV dominates (83% of generation):**
Koundoul at 12 deg N latitude receives high annual solar irradiance. The levelized cost of PV energy in this configuration ($270/panel with 17% efficiency and 20-year lifetime) is significantly lower than the effective cost of wind energy per kWh delivered. The optimization naturally weights solar capacity higher.

**Why battery is large but hydrogen is supplementary:**
Batteries provide fast, efficient (85%) response to short-term deficits. Hydrogen has lower round-trip efficiency (74% electrolysis * 50% FC = ~37%) but provides seasonal-scale storage. The algorithm uses batteries as first storage defense, hydrogen as seasonal backup. The 221,300 kWh battery discharge supports ~150 days of average daily load from batteries alone, while 83,874 kWh of hydrogen energy (~2,516 kg stored in 997 1-kg tanks) covers extended low-renewable periods.

**Why DG = 0 kWh:**
The combined PV+wind+battery+FC system is designed to always meet the load. The LOLP of 0% confirms no hours of unmet load occur at this sizing. The DG is specified as a component but sized to zero -- the renewable+storage system is cheaper and more reliable than incorporating diesel, especially given that diesel fuel must be transported to rural Chad at high cost.

### 5.3 Critical Evaluation

**Reasonable assumptions:**
- 10% real interest rate is consistent with SSA capital costs
- 20-year project lifetime matches PV/turbine warranties
- $1/L diesel price is appropriate for rural Chad delivered pricing
- 100% renewable fraction target aligns with the study's development goals

**Limitations:**
1. **Load profile rigidity:** The paper uses a single representative daily profile; actual loads vary day-to-day and seasonally. The 7.5-hour zero-load window (1 AM - 7:30 AM) may not persist as demand grows with electrification.
2. **NOCT approximation:** Using fixed 45 degC NOCT introduces temperature estimation error.
3. **Electrolyzer/FC lifetime:** Only 5 years for both electrolyzer and fuel cell means multiple replacement cycles over the 20-year project. The model captures this via CRF + replacement costs, but the replacement logistics in rural Chad are not addressed.
4. **MOPSO convergence:** The paper describes a "novel MOPSO" but details on convergence criteria, swarm size, and iteration count are sparse.
5. **Wind data quality:** 4.1 m/s average wind speed is modest; wind turbine output at such low speeds is sensitive to the power curve model and hub height assumptions.
6. **Hydrogen safety:** Storing nearly 1000 kg of hydrogen in rural Chad presents significant safety and maintenance challenges not discussed.
7. **No sensitivity analysis reported in depth:** Variables tested not detailed in the results section.

**Generalizability:**
The methodology (MOPSO for hybrid system sizing) is generalizable. The specific optimal sizes are site-dependent (solar/wind resource, load profile, component costs). The finding that 100% renewable off-grid systems can undercut Chad's national utility production costs may apply broadly across Chad's rural areas, though local resource availability will shift the PV/Wind ratio.

### 5.5 Key Takeaways

1. **100% renewable off-grid electricity is cheaper than the grid in rural Chad.** At $0.2982/kWh, the hybrid PV/Wind/Battery/FC system undercuts Chad's national rural production cost ($0.61/kWh) by 51%, demonstrating that rural electrification via renewables is economically superior to grid extension via the national utility (SNE).

2. **Hydrogen storage complements, rather than replaces, seasonal balancing.** The system achieves 100% RES through monthly complementarity: solar dominates year-round, wind fills gaps during the rainy season (Jun-Nov), batteries manage daily cycles, and hydrogen captures surpluses in wind-solar high-output months. The battery handles most daily storage (221,300 kWh vs 83,874 kWh for hydrogen) -- hydrogen is seasonal reserve, not primary storage.

3. **Multi-objective optimization reveals a quantifiable cost-reliability trade-off.** The Pareto front maps the SSSI vs ASC relationship: pushing from 45% to 57% SSSI costs an additional ~$4,300/year in annualized cost (from $153,891 to $158,177 at the extremes, with the balanced point at $156,034/51.14%). Decision-makers can select their preferred point on this curve.

4. **Social criteria (jobs, HDI) are quantifiable in energy system optimization.** The study demonstrates that energy access planning can incorporate job creation (5 direct jobs per 803 kW installed) and Human Development Index improvement (from 0.398 to 0.4683, a 17.66% increase) as optimization variables alongside economic and technical criteria.

5. **The diesel generator is economically unnecessary in this configuration.** At current diesel ($1/L) and component costs, the optimal sizing eliminates the DG entirely. The combined cost of sufficient PV+wind+battery+FC+hydrogen storage to guarantee 0% LOLP is lower than incorporating any diesel generation. This is a significant finding for Sub-Saharan Africa where diesel generators are often the default rural electricity solution.
